Health insurance vs health plan

Many people use the two terms as synonyms, but they are different products.

ASF

Health insurance contracts are regulated — a discount plan is not regulated in the same way.

Network

In the contracted network, the insurer pays the provider; only the co-payment remains.

Reimbursement

Outside the network, you pay first and then claim reimbursement up to the policy limit.

Without a policy

You can also book a one-off consultation, without taking out annual insurance.

In practice

How the two access modes work

Contracted network

You use providers with an agreement with the insurer. The insurer pays the provider directly — you only pay any co-payment set out in the policy.

Reimbursement

You use the provider you want, pay out of pocket, and then claim reimbursement from the insurer up to the contracted limit. Deadlines and the reimbursement percentage vary from plan to plan.

Before comparing prices: confirm whether the consultation or test you need is in the network, whether there is a co-payment, and whether there is an annual outpatient cover limit. A lower premium can hide tight limits.

Cover

What is usually included in basic plans

  • Hospital inpatient care — the insured capital varies, from about €25,000 to unlimited depending on the plan
  • Consultations, tests and outpatient treatment — network access or a fixed annual limit
  • Online medicine / telemedicine — increasingly common to include at no extra cost for the insured person

The small print

What is usually left out or limited

  • Waiting periods — waiting time after you take out the policy before certain cover comes into force
  • Pre-existing diseases or conditions — often excluded, except in specific plans (rare on the market)
  • Childbirth — frequently an additional option, not included in the basic plan
  • Mental health — increasingly covered, but not always on the entry-level plan
  • Dentistry / dental care — usually requires specific optional cover
  • Clinical assistance while travelling / abroad — not always included in the most basic plan
Note: exclusions and waiting periods are in the General Conditions and the Insurance Product Information Document. Always confirm those documents before comparing quotes in public or signing.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Do I need medical exams to take out health insurance?
Usually you are asked for an Individual Health Questionnaire or a telephone medical interview — not physical exams, except in specific cases.
Can I change plan with the same insurer?
Yes, but generally with a minimum notice period (often 90 days before renewal).
Does health insurance cover travel-medicine consultations?
It depends on the plan. Some policies include online medicine and, in those cases, a travel consultation may be covered at no extra cost — but only for insured people and only if the policy has that cover. It is not a one-off service open to anyone without the insurance. Always confirm the General Conditions. If the plan does not include it, or if you do not yet have insurance, you can book the consultation separately.
