Bangkok, the northern hills, the southern islands — in Thailand **the route changes everything**. A beach-and-city trip needs little. A month of backpacking in the north, or jungle trails near the border, needs a good deal more. That is true whether you are visiting from abroad or you live in Portugal and fly out from here.

A [travel consultation](/marcar/travel) (€39, in English) matches the day-by-day itinerary to your record and says what you need — and what you do not.

## Quick summary

- **Required on entry (flight from Portugal):** no vaccine. Yellow fever is required only if you arrive from a risk country (for example after time in [Brazil](/blog/travel-vaccines-brazil) or in Africa).
- **Malaria:** risk mainly in **border provinces** (Myanmar, Cambodia, Malaysia). In Bangkok, Chiang Mai and Phuket, cases are rare and tablets are not recommended.
- **Japanese encephalitis:** recommended for stays of **a month or more**, or short trips with a lot of time in rural areas.
- **Other vaccines:** hepatitis A and hepatitis B for most people; typhoid and rabies depending on the trip.
- **When to book:** 6 to 8 weeks ahead, especially if you need Japanese encephalitis (two doses).

## Malaria: it depends where you go

According to the CDC:

- **Main risk:** provinces bordering Myanmar, Cambodia (except Buri Ram) and Malaysia, especially rural and forest areas. Preventive tablets are recommended here (atovaquone-proguanil, doxycycline or tafenoquine). The prescription comes from the consultation: [malaria prophylaxis](/consulta/profilaxia-malaria).
- **Rare cases:** Bangkok, Chiang Mai, Chiang Rai, and the islands of Koh Samui, Koh Pha Ngan and Phuket. Bite prevention only, no tablets.
- **No transmission:** Pattaya and the islands of Krabi province (Koh Phi Phi, Koh Lanta, Koh Yao Noi, Koh Yao Yai).

In short: for the classic Bangkok–Chiang Mai–islands circuit, you usually **do not need malaria tablets**. If you are trekking or staying near the borders, you need a medical assessment.

## Japanese encephalitis

Japanese encephalitis is a mosquito-borne brain infection, present across much of Thailand outside the capital. Outbreaks are more frequent in the north (Chiang Mai valley) between May and October, but there have been cases in travellers on the southern coast.

The CDC recommends the vaccine if you:

- stay **a month or more** in areas with Japanese encephalitis;
- are **moving to live** in Thailand;
- travel **often** to these areas.

It suggests considering it on shorter trips with higher-risk activities: rural areas, trekking, camping, or lodging without air conditioning or a net. It is not recommended for short city-only trips.

The vaccine is two doses, so **you need time** — the main reason to book the consultation early. It is given at an [International Vaccination Centre in Portugal](/blog/international-vaccination-centres-portugal), with a prescription.

## Dengue

Dengue circulates throughout Thailand, including cities and islands. Protect yourself from bites during the day: that is when the dengue mosquito bites most. Ask in the consultation whether dengue vaccine makes sense in your case.

## Yellow fever: only if you come from another country

Flying from Portugal, you do not need a certificate. If you arrive from a risk country — for example [Brazil](/blog/travel-vaccines-brazil) or an African country with transmission — the certificate can be required. Vaccine guide: [yellow fever](/blog/vacina-febre-amarela-guia-completo). [It is not given at CUF](/blog/vacina-febre-amarela-cuf).

## Other vaccines to consider

- **Hepatitis A:** strongly recommended.
- **Hepatitis B:** strongly recommended, especially on long stays, with tattoos, or with medical or dental treatment on the ground.
- **Typhoid:** if you eat well outside tourist circuits or go to rural areas.
- **Rabies:** dogs and monkeys are common; consider it for long stays, trails, or contact with animals.
- **Routine vaccines:** measles and flu in particular. If your record is from outside Portugal, bring it.

More context: [malaria and hepatitis A/B](/blog/malaria-hepatite-a-b-viajante).

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need malaria tablets for Phuket or Koh Samui?

Usually no. The CDC recommends bite prevention only on these islands.

### Do I need Japanese encephalitis vaccine for a two-week holiday?

In most cases, no — it depends on what you will do. If you spend a lot of time in rural areas or go trekking, decide in the consultation.

### Do I need any required vaccine?

No, if you travel from Portugal. Only if you arrive from a yellow fever risk country.
