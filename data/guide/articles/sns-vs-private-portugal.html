Two systems

They are not competitors — you can use both

In Portugal, healthcare is divided into two systems that operate independently: the private system and the public system (SNS). They are not competitors in the strict sense — you can use both, at the same time or according to the situation — but they have completely different access and funding models. It is worth understanding clearly how each one is organised before you decide where to seek care.

Private system

Three types of response

The private system is made up of independent services that anyone can access — resident or not, whether or not you pay tax in Portugal — freely, by paying a fee (one-off, through insurance, or through an access plan).

1. Private online consultations

  • Travel consultation (advice before trips, recommended vaccination)
  • Mental health consultation (psychology, psychiatry)
  • General medical consultation, without warning signs and without the need for a physical examination

2. In-person consultations

At independent clinics or private hospitals — the best-known groups in Portugal are CUF, Hospital da Luz and Lusíadas Saúde — for situations that need a physical examination, in-person diagnostic tests, or ongoing follow-up.

3. Private emergency care, inpatient stays and surgery

The same hospital groups (and other smaller or regional ones) also offer a private emergency service, inpatient care and surgery — usually with much shorter waiting times than the public sector, but always paid (one-off or covered by health insurance).

Public system

How the SNS is organised

The National Health Service (SNS) is universally free of charge — which means free for everyone, not only for those who contribute to funding it. It is supported by the amount deducted from citizens in tax, but that does not prevent people who do not pay tax (tourists, migrants in an irregular situation, and so on) from using certain services, such as emergency care.

The SNS is organised into three main components:

  • Hospital emergency care — attendance by priority triage (Manchester Protocol, below)
  • Primary healthcare — family doctor, nurse, psychologist, and so on, available at health centres, usually the first point of contact with the system
  • Hospital or continuing care — specialist consultations, inpatient stays, surgery and long-term care, generally by referral from primary care

Manchester Protocol

In SNS emergency departments, the order of attendance is not the order of the queue: it is the order of severity. The Manchester Protocol assigns a colour and a target time.

RedImmediate · life-threatening
OrangeWithin 10 minutes
YellowWithin 60 minutes
GreenWithin 120 minutes
BlueWithin 240 minutes

A “green” complaint may wait two hours or more. That does not mean the service refused to treat you — it means there are more urgent cases ahead. If the complaint is not an emergency and you do not want to wait, you can book a same-day online consultation.

Tourists

If you are a tourist and need assistance, where can you go?

Public system: emergency care

It is free at the point of care for citizens of the EU/EEA/Switzerland/United Kingdom who hold a European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) — you pay on the same terms as a Portuguese resident (that is, at no cost, since moderating fees in emergency departments were abolished in 2022). The cost of treatment is then settled directly between the Portuguese health system and that of the country of origin, through European agreements — you do not pay that difference yourself.

For tourists from outside the EU/EEA, access to emergency care is not refused for lack of documents (an emergency is always attended), but, without an EHIC or a bilateral agreement with the country of origin, it is normal to be charged for the attendance.

If you are European, you can (and should) use the European Health Insurance Card. Always carry it in your wallet or on your phone, together with your identification document.

SNS 24 or 112?

Before going to an emergency department, the most appropriate step is to contact the SNS 24 line first (808 24 24 24) — the team carries out telephone triage and directs you to the most suitable response, including, if necessary, to the nearest emergency department according to your symptoms. This prior contact is not currently mandatory for most emergency departments — it has only been required for obstetrics and gynaecology emergencies, nationwide, since February 2026. Outside that specific case, it remains a recommendation (to speed up attendance), not an entry requirement. In any life-threatening situation, direct contact is always still 112, without going through telephone triage.

Private system (paid by you)

Online consultation — for mild complaints, with same-day attendance. Clinic, private hospital or private emergency department: contact or go directly to the location.

Long stay

You are an expat, digital nomad, and so on — you intend to stay for a long period

If you plan to stay in Portugal for an extended period, you essentially have two options, which are not mutually exclusive:

  • Option 1: stay in the private system — through private health insurance or paying as you go, without depending on the SNS. This is usually the faster option while your residence situation is not yet regularised.
  • Option 2: obtain an SNS user number (Número de Utente, NNU) — as soon as you have a residence permit (or more than 90 days of documented stay), you can register with the SNS and gain access on the same terms as a Portuguese resident, while keeping private care in parallel for whatever you prefer to treat more quickly.

How to obtain an SNS user number (NNU)

SituationHow to obtain it
Portuguese citizenAssigned automatically with the Citizen Card, printed on the reverse
Brazilian citizenPB4 certificate
Foreign national with a residence permitPresent the Residence Permit at the local health centre
Foreign national in Portugal for more than 90 daysCertificate of Residence (obtained at the local parish council, Junta de Freguesia)
NewbornsAssigned automatically at the hospital, through the “Nascer Cidadão” process

The request is always free of charge. Since 2022, moderating fees have been abolished for consultations, emergency care and tests in the SNS — only some specific situations (such as physiotherapy) retain a residual cost.

Important note: the SNS user number is not the same as the NIF or the NISS — they are three different identifiers, even though they are grouped on the Citizen Card. Having an SNS user number also does not automatically guarantee an assigned family doctor or exemption from moderating fees — that depends on each person's specific situation.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Does the European Health Insurance Card cover vaccines?
No. The EHIC only covers care considered clinically necessary during your stay — vaccination is treated as preventive care and is excluded. That is why complementary support for vaccination before a trip still makes sense, even if you have an EHIC.
Can I use the EHIC at a private clinic in Portugal?
No — it is only accepted in public hospitals and SNS health centres. It also does not cover repatriation and does not replace travel insurance.
Is the EHIC only for emergencies?
In practice, yes, that is mainly what it is for: emergencies and sudden illness during your stay. It also covers continuity of treatment for chronic or pre-existing conditions you already had before travelling, but it never covers planned or elective treatment — you cannot use the EHIC to go to another country on purpose for scheduled surgery or treatment.
Should I call 112 or SNS 24?
112 is only for real life-threatening emergencies — direct contact, without going through telephone triage. SNS 24 (808 24 24 24) is for advice and triage in worrying but non-emergency situations.
Can a tourist use the SNS in Portugal?
Tourists from EU/EEA countries can use the European Health Insurance Card in public emergency departments and health centres. Tourists from third countries generally do not have free access and should use private care or travel insurance.
How long does it take to get an SNS family doctor?
It depends a great deal on the region — in March 2026, more than 1.6 million users in Portugal had no assigned family doctor, a number that has been rising.
Can I use the SNS even if I have private health insurance?
Yes — the SNS is independent of being enrolled in another system (private insurance, ADSE, and so on).
