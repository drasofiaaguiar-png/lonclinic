Angola is a destination where medical preparation is not optional. Yellow fever vaccine is **required to enter the country**, wherever you fly from, and malaria exists across the whole territory, including Luanda. Work, family, or a holiday: deal with this in time. That includes people who live in Portugal and fly out from Lisbon or Porto.

A [travel consultation](/marcar/travel) (€39, in English) sets the plan and issues the prescription. Yellow fever is given at an [International Vaccination Centre in Portugal](/blog/international-vaccination-centres-portugal) — [not at CUF](/blog/vacina-febre-amarela-cuf).

## Quick summary

- **Required on entry:** a <strong>yellow fever</strong> vaccination certificate for every traveller aged 9 months or older.
- **Malaria:** risk <strong>country-wide</strong>. The CDC recommends preventive tablets.
- **Other vaccines to consider:** hepatitis A, typhoid, hepatitis B, rabies, meningitis.
- **When to book:** 4 to 8 weeks ahead. Yellow fever must be given **at least 10 days before** arrival.

## Yellow fever: required

According to the CDC, Angola requires proof of yellow fever vaccination for **all travellers aged 9 months or older**, whatever the country of departure. Without the International Certificate of Vaccination, you can have problems on arrival.

- The vaccine is given at **International Vaccination Centres**. [See the centres in Portugal](/blog/international-vaccination-centres-portugal). By city: [Lisbon](/blog/vacina-febre-amarela-lisboa), [Porto](/blog/vacina-febre-amarela-porto), [Coimbra](/blog/vacina-febre-amarela-coimbra).
- The certificate is valid only **10 days after the vaccine**.
- One dose is, for most people, valid for life. If you were already vaccinated, take the original certificate.
- Angola had major yellow fever outbreaks in 2016, which led to cases in unvaccinated travellers.
- If you have a medical reason not to take the vaccine (for example certain conditions that affect immunity), the doctor assesses that and, if appropriate, issues an exemption.

**Watch the onward route:** if you continue from Angola to another country (for example [Mozambique](/blog/travel-vaccines-mozambique) or [India](/blog/travel-vaccines-india)), having been in Angola can make the certificate required at the next destination too.

Vaccine guide: [yellow fever](/blog/vacina-febre-amarela-guia-completo).

## Malaria: risk everywhere

Malaria exists in every region of Angola, and it is mainly *Plasmodium falciparum*, the most severe form. The parasites are resistant to chloroquine, so other medicines are used: atovaquone-proguanil, doxycycline, mefloquine or tafenoquine.

The choice depends on length of stay, your health, other medicines, and pregnancy. It is a medical decision — and the medicine needs a prescription: [malaria prophylaxis](/consulta/profilaxia-malaria).

As well as the tablets:

- Use repellent, especially in the late afternoon and at night.
- Sleep under an insecticide-treated net, or in a room with air conditioning.
- **Any fever during the trip or in the months after you return is a medical emergency.** Always tell the doctor you were in Angola.

## Other vaccines to consider

- **Hepatitis A:** for most travellers.
- **Typhoid:** especially family visits, rural areas, or long stays.
- **Hepatitis B:** long stays, healthcare work, or a chance of medical treatment on the ground.
- **Rabies:** Angola is considered high risk; preventive vaccine is advised for long stays and contact with animals.
- **Meningitis:** in specific situations, decided in the consultation.
- **Routine vaccines:** measles, tetanus and polio. If your record is from outside Portugal, bring it.

More context: [malaria and hepatitis A/B](/blog/malaria-hepatite-a-b-viajante).

## How far ahead to book

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| 6–8 weeks before | Travel consultation and the start of multi-dose vaccines |
| At least 10 days before | Yellow fever vaccine (required) |
| 1–2 weeks before | Collect malaria tablets |
| Last minute | Still get in touch — yellow fever is the critical point |

Because yellow fever vaccine is required and the centres often have waiting lists, **do not leave it to the last week**. The [online consultation](/marcar/travel) means you arrive at the centre with the prescription.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is yellow fever vaccine really required for Angola?

Yes, for every traveller aged 9 months or older.

### I was vaccinated more than 10 years ago. Do I need a booster?

In most cases, no: since 2016 the certificate is valid for life. There are exceptions, assessed in the consultation.

### I am only going to Luanda. Do I need malaria tablets?

Yes. The risk exists across the whole country, including Luanda.

### I am Angolan and I live in Portugal. I am visiting family. Do I still need this?

Yes. Time living outside a malaria area reduces the partial protection people sometimes assume they still have, and the entry rules apply the same way.
